# Changelog

One entry per release, newest first. Releases are published from the Desktop workflow; the installer and update files are on the GitHub release page. Entries before 30.3.0 are written from the commit history, and describe what was built; some of it was first tried on a real PC only afterwards.

## Next release (not published)
- The motion added in the last round now runs across the app, not only in the header and the video card: the heart (with its burst) on the performer/studio favourite button, the watch page, the photo viewer, the photo badge and both "Favourites" filters; icon swap on the slideshow, fullscreen, play/pause and mute buttons; the typing and dissolve search box on the channel picker, the quick-edit and bulk-edit lists, the studio mapping list and the country list; the accordion fold on every collapsible section (scene markers, job details, check details, match details, Telegram recovery, runtime report); an unfolding reveal for the player menus and the country list; error shake on the profile and channel name fields.

- **Video favourites (the star on the Videos page, and the bulk favourite) are answered by the Rust front door**, with the same answers and the same database changes as Python (write parity: 51 of 51 scenarios identical). Rust's "cross-site request refused" answer now uses Python's wording.
- **Fix (a Telegram channel-to-channel transfer was stopped by the watchdog right after preparation, from the 15 Nov log, 2 of 2 attempts):** after the Metadata & Assets preparation the transfer reads the source messages again, 100 per Telegram call. Telegram answered each call with a 20 to 30 s FloodWait (the account had just been used heavily by the preparation), and the loop printed nothing until it finished, so the job showed no activity for two minutes and the watchdog force-stopped it. Every call is now preceded by a progress line ("Reading source messages from Telegram: 300 of 1,252"), which counts as activity. **Not fixed, only found:** 4 of the 25 videos could not get playback snapshots in both runs ("Fetched GOP range does not contain all requested samples", "Invalid MP4 NAL length", one local 5XX). That looks like a short fetch of the tail of the file, but the log does not show the cause.
- **Three animations from Transitions.dev (free for commercial use; credited in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`; the CSS is in `apps/web/src/design/transitions.css`, the published snippets unchanged):**
  - **Like button:** the star on a video card is now a heart. Liking fills it red with a spring pop and a burst of eight small dots that goes a different way every time; unliking empties it quietly. A card that is already liked when the page loads shows the filled heart without playing the animation. The star remains on the video page's Favourite button and on performer and studio pages.
  - **Notification badge:** the notifications button in the top row is now a bell (it was a clock), and its count slides onto the bell's corner and pops in, and shrinks away when the list is empty. The bell itself does not move, and the number stays readable while the badge closes.
  - **Icon swap:** the sidebar collapse and expand button is one button now, and its two arrows blur and scale into each other in the same spot instead of one button replacing the other. In the collapsed sidebar the button sits under the logo.
  - All three follow the system's "reduce motion" setting and play no animation when it is on.
- **More motion, from Transitions.dev (credited, CSS unchanged in `apps/web/src/design/transitions.css`) and SmoothUI (MIT):**
  - **Search box (top bar):** three things. (1) Typing: every new letter springs in and a caret glides between letters (the idea of unlumen UI's "Animated Input", which is a paid component: I did not have its code and wrote this from its public description). (2) Clearing with the × dissolves the text: it flies down, blurs and fades while a soft streak lights up under each word and the placeholder falls in from above (Transitions.dev "Input clear with dissolve"). Escape closes the suggestions first and clears on the second press. (3) Suggestions, like a favicon search (SmoothUI): while you type, matching performers and studios are listed with their picture (a letter tile when there is none), the typed letters marked, arrow keys and Enter to pick one and open its page; Enter with nothing highlighted keeps filtering the current page as before. The lists behind it load the first time you use the box.
  - **Theme toggle:** the top-bar button is SmoothUI's sun and moon (the sun's rays fold in and a mask carves the disc into a crescent), and the "Dark theme" row in the account menu is its night-sky switch with stars and a lit knob. Light and dark only.
  - **Dropdown menus open like an accordion** (Transitions.dev): the video card menu, photo card menu, selection-bar menus, the account menu and every drop-down list (the themed select) grow and fold with a short blur and fade, and the arrow on a menu button flips while it is open. A menu still opens above its button when there is no room below.
  - **Error shake:** a rejected input shakes left and right with a small overshoot and gets a red ring, and its message fades in underneath: a wrong password (or a mismatched confirmation) on the sign-in form, an invalid performer or studio name in the editor, an empty or invalid title in "Edit title", and a wrong Telegram login code or cloud password. The ring stays until you type again, so the message can be read.
  - All of it follows the system's reduce-motion setting.
- **Photos page: the row of people scrolls sideways with the mouse wheel, by dragging with the mouse, with two arrow buttons, and by touch.** The row had no scroll bar, so on a PC it could not be moved at all. The wheel moves it sideways while the pointer is over it (and goes back to scrolling the page at either end); dragging with the mouse moves it without opening the person under the pointer at the end of the drag; the arrows appear only on the side that has more people; touch keeps the browser's own swipe. The arrow keys also work when the row is focused. The same scroller (`design/HScroll.tsx`) can be used for other rows.
- **Counters on the sidebar (and the phone bar) show what is running on each page.** A small pulsing number appears next to a page while jobs for that page are working, and disappears when they stop: Transfers (transfers, media manager, local uploads, content automation), Videos (metadata and asset runs, snapshots, suggestion scans, and a video that is playing), Performers and Studios (performer and channel data), Settings (backups), and Operations (every running job). Paused, queued and interrupted jobs are not counted. Photos and AI show nothing yet: their work does not run as jobs, so it is not in the live status.
- **Live-updates dot (the small dot on your picture): when it is red, the log now says why.** Green means the live connection to the backend is up, amber that it is connecting, red that it was lost and is retrying; it is not the Telegram status (that is the "Connected" pill). Each time the connection closes, the window now reports the close code, the reason, how long it had been open and the retry count to the diagnostics (at most once a minute), and the logs zip includes it. I could not reproduce a red dot here: the connection opens through the Rust front door and stays up.
- **Performers page: the Trans filter button is gone.** The filter is now Female, Male and All. Performers whose gender is trans are not deleted or changed; they show under All.
- **Videos, Performers and Studios pages: the last row of cards is always full.** The page sizes were fixed at 12, 24, 36 and 48, and the number of columns depends on the window (5 on a wide screen), so 12 cards filled two rows and left two cards on a third. The page now measures its grid and rounds the page size up to whole rows: with 5 columns the choices read 15, 25, 40 and 50, with 4 columns 12, 24, 36 and 48. They follow the window as it is resized or the scale changes. The final page of a list can still end part-way, because that depends on how many items there are.
- **No more "Restore from provider data" and "Fetch now" bars on the Performers and Studios pages; genders and pictures are filled in by the app itself.**
  - **When:** at the end of the performer and studio phases of every Metadata & Assets run (also when those phases are skipped because the entities are "fresh"), when the Performers or Studios page is opened, and every ten minutes while it stays open. Opening the page repeatedly runs it at most once per ten minutes. The lists refresh by themselves after 20 and 90 seconds.
  - **Why the buttons never seemed to work:** (1) a fetch that failed for a passing reason (network error, a download that did not complete) was remembered for a week, so pressing Fetch again did nothing and the count never went down; such failures are now retried after 6 hours and only "the provider has no picture" waits a week. (2) The button said nothing about what happened; every pass now writes one line to the log with the outcome counts ("5 found, 20 not available (25 tried: 14 no_provider_artwork, 6 found, 5 error)"), so the next logs zip shows why a picture could not be found. (3) The gender restore only read the details stored with each performer's videos; it now also reads the provider record stored by "Collect performer data".
  - **Still true:** a performer or studio that StashDB and ThePornDB have no picture or gender for stays without one; the log line above says how many that is. Genders can still be set by hand in the edit dialog.
- **Duplicate performer cards: the log now says why each one was created.** Archive X creates a second performer record for an existing name on purpose in two cases: the same provider (StashDB, say) gave the existing record a different id, so it might be a different person; or several existing records match and none can be chosen. Merging them by hand treats the symptom. Every time this happens the log now gets a warning naming the case and the ids and provider ids involved, so the next logs zip shows which case made your duplicates and the matching rule can be fixed for that case instead of guessed at.
- **Fix (writer):** if a database commit failed, for example because another process held the lock, the Rust writer stayed inside the open transaction and every later write failed until the app restarted. It now rolls back.

## 30.2.7 (2026-11-15)

- **Jobs no longer lose their settings, and no longer get stopped while checking snapshots (two causes found in the 30.2.6 log):**
  - The metadata-match stage finishes by writing a progress sentence ("fingerprints 0/21 ok") into the job's row, where the channel pipeline keeps the settings it restarts from. After that, Recover (or resuming after a restart) failed at once with "Select at least one source channel". A progress sentence can no longer replace those settings.
  - Before making snapshots, Archive X checks about 40 local files for every video in the channel. On a large channel that takes minutes and said nothing, so the job's watchdog stopped the whole job after 60 s of silence. The check now reports progress every 200 videos.
- **Crash trace:** if the process dies inside native code (the video decoder, an image library), the stack of every thread is now written to `native-crash.log` in the log folder, which the log zip includes. The previous session in the last log simply stopped in the middle of decoding a snapshot frame, and nothing said why.
- **StashDB fingerprint lookups work again:** StashDB removed the query Archive X used for file fingerprints (`findScenesByFullFingerprints`), so every OSHASH lookup failed with HTTP 422 (21 of 21 in the last log: "0 matched, 21 failed"). The lookup now uses the current query.
- **Freezes, part 3:** the channel scan held the database lock for the whole batch, so the app waited for all of it (a 95 s freeze, after which the watchdog stopped the job); it now holds the lock for one record at a time. Two queries over every linked performer moved off the thread that serves the app (13 s).
- **Faster start:** the clean-up of old cache leftovers (11 s of an 18 s start) runs beside the start instead of before it.

- **Fewer freezes, part 2:** the similar-name search in the performer identity check skips names that share too few letters to be similar (about 6 times faster again, the same answers); the diagnostics snapshot no longer asks Windows for the python processes on the thread that serves the app (it froze the app for 8 s).

## 30.2.6 (2026-11-15)

- **Fewer freezes (from a real log):** the log of 30.2.4 showed the whole app freezing for 8 to 15 seconds, 13 times in about 5 hours, once for 202 seconds. Two causes found and fixed: the performer identity check read every performer (biography included) and compared names against all of them on every call, on the thread that serves the app (now an index kept until the performers change; the answers are the same, tested against the old algorithm; about 6 times faster on the first call and far more after, measured on 30,000 performers); and the channel scan wrote thousands of rows, with a commit each, on that same thread (now on a worker thread). A swallowed error that appeared 22 times (`DirectTelegramRangeSource` had no `close`) is fixed.

- **Removed (backend):** the second way to run the Python server, which the desktop app never used: HTTPS with a local certificate authority, access from other devices on the network, HTTP/3, the phone certificate download (`/ca.crt`) and the `hypercorn` dependency. `python -m telegram_archive` now always runs the loopback desktop mode and prints the hand-off link when started by hand. The installer's backend is a little smaller.

## 30.2.5 (2026-11-15)

- **Fix (the Rust routes in 30.2.4 did not start in the real desktop app):** the front door's own question to the backend ("where is the database?") was refused by the backend's desktop guard, so in 30.2.4 every route that reads the database stayed with Python (nothing broke, nothing was faster). The front door now sends the launch secret with its own calls, and routes it answers itself require the launch secret or cookie, as the backend does (found by running the front door in front of a backend in desktop mode, which the earlier tests did not).

- **Sign-in check:** the performer name list and face learning check the session in the database (as the other Rust routes do) instead of asking Python on every request.

## 30.2.4 (2026-11-15)

- **Native player:** the video's own window is the default layer (the one that showed the picture on a real PC); Back no longer freezes the app; less lag from the on-screen check; a quicker start on network streams; jumps show where they are going.
- **Playback:** jumping into the middle of an MP4 that the browser plays through the in-browser remux now works; the player offers the native player when the browser cannot decode a file; leaving a video releases its server job.
- **Performers and studios:** missing pictures are fetched in the background (and with a "Fetch now" button); SVG logos with a standard editor doctype are accepted; a button restores genders that editing a picture used to wipe; searching accepts part of a name in any order.
- **People:** the name search can be cancelled and only one is open at a time.
- **Performance and stability:** the performer page no longer freezes the whole app; the AI page no longer looks empty while it loads; the watchdog guardian, which could not start in the installed app, is off there; reopening the app straight after closing it works.
- **Rust front door (all on in the desktop app):** the Videos list, tags, photos list, studio details, favourites, scene markers and watch progress are answered by the Rust front door instead of Python (the front door finds the database through the backend; `ARCHIVE_X_NATIVE_ROUTES=none` switches them off). Rust now writes watch history, performer and studio favourites and scene markers (ADR 0007). A race that could stop the first performer-name refresh on Windows ("database is locked") is fixed.
- **Licence:** GPL-3.0-or-later (ADR 0008); `LICENSE` and `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` are in the repository and the installer.
- **Housekeeping:** one command runs every test; one version number; unused frontend code removed; a counter records which API routes are used (for the clean-up); documents reorganised under `docs/`.
