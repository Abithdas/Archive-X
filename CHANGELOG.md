# Changelog

One entry per release, newest first. Releases are published from the Desktop workflow; the installer and update files are on the GitHub release page. Entries before 30.3.0 are written from the commit history, and describe what was built; some of it was first tried on a real PC only afterwards.

## Next release (not published)
- Conflicting identifications are now settled in one place (new `metadata/evidence_resolver.py`, used by the scene-match step): a provider candidate is weighed against what the video's own snapshots say (the watermark studio and the faces).
  - A file-fingerprint (OSHASH) match is authoritative: never downgraded, only annotated when the snapshots disagree.
  - One contradiction (the watermark names another studio, or the faces are performers not in the scene's cast) caps the candidate at "needs review". Two contradictions drop it to LOW. A studio's network or alias name is not a contradiction.
  - A MEDIUM candidate is applied automatically only when the watermark AND a face both agree with it, nothing contradicts it and its own score is solid (0.60 or more). Snapshot evidence alone never applies anything, and a rejected suggestion is not evidence.
  - Videos that have snapshots but were not looked at yet are looked at when a candidate is found, so the evidence exists at decision time.
- People recognition from your own videos, and use gallery pictures as the profile picture (new):
  - The snapshots of the videos mapped to a performer are searched for their face. Clear, sharp faces (at least 56 px wide) that match the performer become Gallery pictures labelled "From a video" (at most 12 per performer, 2 per video, no near-duplicates). Because the gallery already feeds the People check, recognition improves with every video.
  - Which face counts: with reference faces already, one that matches them and beats every other performer by a clear margin. With none yet, only a video where they are the only mapped performer, and only the one face seen in at least 6 snapshots.
  - Removing such a picture ("not this performer") is final: it is not taken again, faces that look like it are skipped, and that video is not searched for them again.
  - Gallery tab: new "Use as profile" on every picture (stored like a hand-uploaded picture and locked, so provider syncs do not replace it) and "Find pictures in videos" to search now. It also runs at the end of each metadata match, and only when the face models are already installed. Pictures from videos no longer count against the 40-picture provider limit.
- Identify videos from their snapshots (new): for videos the providers could not name, Archive X now looks at the video's own 36 snapshots. Suggestions only; nothing changes until you press Save in Edit video, where a new "From snapshots" panel lists them with Add and "Not them", and a "Check snapshots" button looks at that one video now.
  - Studio watermark: the part of each corner that stays put while the picture changes is compared with the watermark learned from that studio's other videos (a studio only gets a mark when at least 3 of its videos, and over half, agree on it). A mark two studios share is never guessed.
  - Performer faces: faces in the snapshots are compared with every performer's profile picture, gallery pictures and confirmed faces. A performer is suggested when they are the clear best match in at least 3 snapshots.
  - It learns: faces from a suggestion you accepted become references, a studio's mark improves with every video you name, "Not them" is remembered for that video, and a video is looked at again only when new marks or references exist.
  - Runs at the end of every metadata match (25 videos per run, so a big library is worked through over several runs). It uses the face models only if People has already installed them; it never downloads anything by itself.
- Telegram → Telegram transfer: new option "Videos without saved snapshots" on the Options step. "Use saved snapshots only" (the default) skips a video that has no saved 36-frame set: it is not made from Telegram, the source video is kept, the job log names it ("SKIPPED — NEEDS SNAPSHOTS: <file> (message N)") and the job summary counts them. The preparation then also leaves out its Snapshots stage. "Make missing snapshots from Telegram first" is the previous behaviour. The choice is saved with the job, so a resumed job keeps it. Review shows the choice.
- Telegram transfer, both ways: new option "Only videos shorter than (minutes)" on the Options step, for Local → Telegram videos and for Telegram → Telegram. Type 10 and only videos under 10 minutes are sent; empty (or 0) means any length. "Under" is strict, so a video exactly 10:00 long is left out, and so is a video whose length cannot be read (it is never guessed). Left-out videos stay where they are. The job log and summary say how many were kept ("[DURATION FILTER] under 10 min: kept 312/4,589 source videos"), the Review step shows the choice, and a resumed Telegram job keeps it. Not offered for image uploads.
- Metadata scan hardening (found by 178 new scenario tests of the channel scan; 21 of them failed before these fixes):
  - A complete full-history scan that found no videos at all (access lost, a Telegram hiccup) marked every stored video of the channel as deleted and removed it from the Library. It now removes nothing, says so in the log and marks the filename step unfinished.
  - A channel with more than about 32,000 videos failed the filename step with "too many SQL variables". Long id lists (filename step, readiness count, performer/studio lookup, entity refresh) are now handled in batches.
  - An empty list of videos meant "every video in the database" in the helpers that finish entity work and count readiness. It now means none.
  - A job whose saved stages were empty or misspelt finished as "completed" having done nothing. It now stops with a clear message.
  - The start route split a channel id sent as one text value into single digits, accepted a name that is not a number, and answered 500 to a malformed prerequisites setting. It now takes one id or a list, refuses non-numbers with a message, and ignores a malformed setting.
  - The progress report the page polls crashed on a saved job with damaged or unusual values (older versions, a write cut short). It now always returns a report.
  - Checked and found sound (tests now keep them so): cancel at every point leaves nothing running and a later run finishes the job; finished stages are not repeated; each stage alone and in combinations; failure of any stage fails only that channel; Telegram rate limits are waited out (5 tries); the daily scan window (overnight, equal times, bad values); the pending-only scan; provider errors, deleted messages and unreadable fingerprints in metadata matching.
- Faster channel scan and identification (measured on a 4,589-video test channel, on a fast SSD; a slow or hard disk should gain more):
  - Fingerprint lookups are batched: StashDB is now asked about 40 videos' file fingerprints in one request instead of one request per video. Provider requests are paced (0.5 s apart for StashDB), so 4,589 separate lookups needed at least 38 minutes; batched they need about 115 requests, around a minute. The answers go into the same cache the per-video lookup reads, so nothing else changes; a reply that is not exactly one answer per file is never trusted, and any failure falls back to the old per-video lookup.
  - Saving the scanned videos is 3 times faster (8.1 s to 2.6 s here): a commit and a fixed pause per video became one commit per ~50 ms of work, while still releasing the database often enough for the rest of the app.
  - The "which videos still need snapshots" check is about 2.4 times faster (5.9 s to 2.5 s) and makes 3 times fewer file-system calls (182,000 to 57,600): each video's frame folder is read once instead of 36 files being checked several times over, and the search for older-version folders no longer scans the whole channel folder once per video. It gives exactly the same answers as before (checked across 13 folder states).
- Performer gallery: every picture the providers (StashDB, ThePornDB) hold of a performer is now downloaded and kept on this PC, and the face matching learns from all of them. Before, the People check used one picture per performer (the profile picture).
  - New **Gallery** tab on a performer's page: the kept pictures (click to enlarge), where each came from, which ones are used for face matching, "Get new pictures" to fetch more, and "Remove" on a picture that is not this performer (it is deleted, never fetched again and no longer used for matching). Pictures come from the same lookup as the profile-picture picker, which only trusts an exact name, an exact alias or a stored provider id.
  - The Check faces job now downloads the galleries of the next batch of named performers (up to 60, at most 10 minutes per run; the rest follow next time and each performer is refreshed monthly), reads the face in each picture, and uses it. A picture serves as a reference only when it shows one clear face, so a photo of two people cannot teach the wrong face.
  - Matching improves as you work: the references for a performer are their profile picture, their gallery pictures and up to twelve varied faces you have confirmed for them (never a face you took out). A performer's score is their best reference, so more pictures can only help a true match; automatic naming still needs a clear margin over the next performer, and the People suggestions list now draws on all of it.
  - Safe by design: pictures are downloaded only from public addresses (never this PC or a local network), at most 8 MB each and 40 per performer, only real photos at least 120 px (JPEG, PNG or WebP), duplicates are dropped, and a removed or unusable picture is remembered. Merging two performers joins their galleries; deleting one deletes their pictures; a picture whose file has gone (a restored backup) is fetched again.
- Identification in one go: a metadata scan asked about each video once, in turn, with what the catalogue knew at that moment. A video recognised later in the same scan adds performers and studios that would have helped an earlier one, and a provider can time out for one video and answer the next. So after a Local Upload (which leaves identification to the destination channel scan) you needed the channel scan, then a "recheck metadata" from the Unknown performer/studio page, then often another, each finding a few more. The matching step now asks again about the videos still unidentified while the previous round identified more (and once after a provider error), up to 3 follow-up rounds, and logs each round ("follow-up round 1 asked about N unidentified video(s) and identified M more"). Videos already identified or sent for review are not asked again, and a pass that learns nothing causes no follow-up. This covers the channel scan and the recheck button, since both use this step.
- Two metadata providers (StashDB first, ThePornDB second), checked with 16 scenarios:
  - Already sound: StashDB is asked first and ThePornDB only when StashDB has no answer or a sparse record; a complete StashDB fingerprint match never calls ThePornDB; with one key configured the other provider is never called; an error from one does not stop the other, and two errors are reported as errors, never as "no match"; the same content on two different websites stays two candidates for review.
  - Fixed: the same performer spelled a little differently by the two providers ("Ava Stone" / "Ava Stones") was listed twice and would have become a second performer row. The other spelling is now folded in as an alias. Accents and "&"/"and" no longer make two names or titles look different, and a studio name in front of a title ("Brazzers - Title") no longer splits one scene into two candidates.
  - Fixed: when each provider names a cast member the other does not ("Mick Blue" vs "Rico Strong"), the merge kept everyone, so a possibly wrong performer was attached. StashDB's cast is now kept, the dispute is recorded, and a scene matched without an exact fingerprint is not applied automatically ("the two providers disagree on the cast"; it stays for review). A provider that simply lists extra people still adds them.
- Video identification, performers and studio (checked with 29 scenarios; 6 failed before these fixes):
  - A matched scene that listed no cast, or no studio, erased the performers or studio the video already had (from its filename or an operator choice), leaving "Unknown Performer / Unknown Studio". Existing links are now replaced only when the match names a cast or studio.
  - ThePornDB cast members arrived without a gender, so those performers had none until a later refresh (the "N still blank" genders in the scan log). The gender is now read when ThePornDB supplies it (best effort; not checked against the live service).
  - A StashDB or ThePornDB reply with an unusual shape (a cast entry that is not an object, a studio sent as plain text, tags that are not a list) crashed the reader, so that video counted as a provider error. Both readers now skip what they cannot read.
  - Checked and sound: cast, studio, tags, title and date are all saved from a match; the same performer in two videos is one row (also across capitals and accents); a cast listing someone twice links them once; the placeholders are replaced by the real cast and studio; a wrong earlier match is corrected; operator mappings and locked fields survive; approving a suggestion keeps known mappings; the performer stage then finds the cast the match linked.
- Video identification: a ThePornDB token saved by an older version under the name `tpdb_token` showed as configured and passed the scan's key check, but the matching read only `tpdb_api_token`, so every lookup answered "No StashDB API key or ThePornDB token saved" and every video stayed unmatched. Both names now count.
- Fix: snapshots no longer fail "at random" on a busy PC. While one video's frames were being fetched from Telegram, other videos' bytes pushed them out of a shared 96 MB memory cache, and the frame failed with "Shared scan byte cache did not cover" (then "Fetched GOP range does not contain all requested samples"), which left the whole video without snapshots ("4 need Snapshots" in a Telegram transfer). The cause: once one video's cached bytes passed 96 MB (around frame 33 of 36 on a big video) the cache threw away the whole video, including what it had just stored, so the last frames failed. The cache now drops other videos first and only the oldest pieces of the same video, and a read also uses the pieces it just downloaded and fetches again only what is missing. A video still needs all 36 real frames; nothing is filled in from a neighbour.
- The snapshot check now says why a video is listed. A scanned video can still be listed as needing snapshots, because the check wants all 36 frame files, a validation receipt, the grid picture, a saved database row that matches the current folder and version, and the playback-snapshot card image. The log now has a line "Snapshot check: N video(s) need snapshot work" naming the first missing thing for each of the first 12 (for example "only 35 of 36 snapshot frames on disk" or "database points at a different snapshot folder"), followed by what the database says about it (in or not in the Library, snapshot state, and the last change, for example STALE_REFRESH_REQUIRED).
- Fix: PyAV was reported "MISSING" on every installed copy (the log said "Archive X is already running with data folder ... Close the running Archive X first"), so every media job quietly used the slower FFmpeg path. The installed backend is one program with no separate Python; the PyAV health check and the isolated media worker were started with `-c` / `-m`, which it ignores, so each one started a second copy of the whole app that stopped at the one-instance lock. They now start with their own flags that the backend answers. A normal worker finish also no longer writes a "worker_error ... SystemExit: 0" line.

## 30.3.0 (2026-11-15)
- Review panels (Scene Match Review, Duplicate review, the earlier automatic matches): click a small picture and it opens to half the window with a 3D tilt that settles flat, so it can be reviewed properly; click again (or press Escape) and it flies back to its place the same way. The video snapshots, the provider scene picture and the thumbnails all do it.
- Deleting now has a smoky dissolve: a deleted video card, a deleted duplicate copy and an approved/rejected/kept review row are displaced and blurred by moving noise while smoke rises from where they were, then everything below slides up to close the gap on the Transitions.dev "Panel reveal" curve (400 ms, soft blur). In the duplicate review each deleted copy goes one by one and the list no longer blanks and reloads between them.

- AI Assistance, set up in one step. The assistant used to need a `llama-server` program and a `.gguf` file chosen by hand, with nothing to download, and the default file name did not match any real download. Now a "Set up" button downloads the llama.cpp engine (about 25 MB) and a model (Small 1.2 GB, Balanced 2.3 GB, Strong 4.7 GB; Balanced is recommended, Small on PCs under about 7 GB of memory), checks both against fixed SHA-256 checksums, resumes if the connection drops, starts the assistant and asks it a test question. On Windows it can use a graphics card (Vulkan). The old manual paths are still there under Models, Advanced.
- The assistant starts by itself when you ask and stops after 20 idle minutes to give the memory back. It waits up to four minutes for a big model to load (it used to give up after 30 seconds) and keeps the engine's own log, so a failed start says why.
- Answers now stream: the page shows what the assistant is doing (starting, thinking, each tool it uses, writing) and the answer arrives word by word, instead of one long wait that could time out at 90 seconds on a slow PC. The default context window is 8192 tokens and the app snapshot is trimmed to fit it (the old 4096 could be smaller than the snapshot sent with every question).
- The AI page is redesigned around this: a first-run setup panel with the real steps, download progress and log; Transitions.dev shimmer text, thinking states, streaming text and the matrix dot loader for the live states; a spinner that turns into a check for each step (written for Archive X; the Transitions.dev version is a paid one).
- People ("Is this the right person?"): answering Yes or No on one card no longer greys out every other card. The answered card steps aside at once and the next one can be answered straight away; each answer goes to the server on its own, and a card comes back with an error message if its answer fails.
- People: a named person who has new look-alike faces waiting now shows a count badge on their card, a "Review new matches" button, a dot on their picture in the People row, and a count on the People tab. (New desktop-app route `/api/photos/faces/learning/review`, read-only.)
- Check faces: new look-alike faces can now be turned down as well as added. Tick the ones that are not the person and press "Not <name>": nothing is moved, but the matching learns from it and stops offering those faces and faces like them. (New backend route `POST /api/photos/faces/learning/not-this-person`.)
- Photos filter: the numbers on the Content and Looks chips now follow what is ticked. Tick Long hair and every other chip shows how many of those long-haired photos also have black hair, breasts and so on; a tag with nothing left is dimmed but stays in place. The counts also follow the channel, search, favourites and face filters. The progress lines ("1,741 of 1,741 photos looked at") stay overall.

- Going back no longer starts over: the Performers and Studios lists keep their page, gender, sort and items-per-page when you open a card and press back, and every page returns to where it was scrolled. A new search still starts at page 1.

## 30.2.7 (2026-11-15)

- **Jobs no longer lose their settings, and no longer get stopped while checking snapshots (two causes found in the 30.2.6 log):**
  - The metadata-match stage finishes by writing a progress sentence ("fingerprints 0/21 ok") into the job's row, where the channel pipeline keeps the settings it restarts from. After that, Recover (or resuming after a restart) failed at once with "Select at least one source channel". A progress sentence can no longer replace those settings.
  - Before making snapshots, Archive X checks about 40 local files for every video in the channel. On a large channel that takes minutes and said nothing, so the job's watchdog stopped the whole job after 60 s of silence. The check now reports progress every 200 videos.
- **Crash trace:** if the process dies inside native code (the video decoder, an image library), the stack of every thread is now written to `native-crash.log` in the log folder, which the log zip includes. The previous session in the last log simply stopped in the middle of decoding a snapshot frame, and nothing said why.
- **StashDB fingerprint lookups work again:** StashDB removed the query Archive X used for file fingerprints (`findScenesByFullFingerprints`), so every OSHASH lookup failed with HTTP 422 (21 of 21 in the last log: "0 matched, 21 failed"). The lookup now uses the current query.
- **Freezes, part 3:** the channel scan held the database lock for the whole batch, so the app waited for all of it (a 95 s freeze, after which the watchdog stopped the job); it now holds the lock for one record at a time. Two queries over every linked performer moved off the thread that serves the app (13 s).
- **Faster start:** the clean-up of old cache leftovers (11 s of an 18 s start) runs beside the start instead of before it.

- **Fewer freezes, part 2:** the similar-name search in the performer identity check skips names that share too few letters to be similar (about 6 times faster again, the same answers); the diagnostics snapshot no longer asks Windows for the python processes on the thread that serves the app (it froze the app for 8 s).

## 30.2.6 (2026-11-15)

- **Fewer freezes (from a real log):** the log of 30.2.4 showed the whole app freezing for 8 to 15 seconds, 13 times in about 5 hours, once for 202 seconds. Two causes found and fixed: the performer identity check read every performer (biography included) and compared names against all of them on every call, on the thread that serves the app (now an index kept until the performers change; the answers are the same, tested against the old algorithm; about 6 times faster on the first call and far more after, measured on 30,000 performers); and the channel scan wrote thousands of rows, with a commit each, on that same thread (now on a worker thread). A swallowed error that appeared 22 times (`DirectTelegramRangeSource` had no `close`) is fixed.
